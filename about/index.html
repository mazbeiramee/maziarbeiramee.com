About

Finance student, founder, and a reader of company histories.

My story

I grew up in Potomac, Maryland, and am a senior at The George Washington University in Washington, DC, studying International Business and Finance. I speak English and Farsi, and my Iranian background has shaped much of how I see the world.

I've always been curious about what makes people and businesses successful. Why do some companies endure for generations while others disappear? What separates exceptional founders from everyone else? And how do seemingly small decisions compound into extraordinary outcomes?

That curiosity has led me to spend much of my free time reading biographies, studying businesses, and learning from people who have built things that last. I'm particularly interested in markets, technology, capital allocation, and human behavior, and in what it means to find great companies and hold them for the long term.

I interned at Guidehouse, working on research and strategy for large public-sector organizations. Much of my work so far has been about understanding businesses from the outside in: sizing markets, studying competitors, and examining how organizations make decisions.

I also wanted to know what it takes to build something myself. I founded Pulseline, a company that built AI voice agents for HVAC businesses. It meant approaching a business from the inside, starting with customer conversations, understanding problems firsthand, and learning how to turn an idea into something people actually want.

Outside of work and school, I enjoy lifting, running, reading, and spending time with friends and family. I'm drawn to people who are curious, ambitious, and willing to challenge how I think.

I'm still early in my career, but I know I want to spend it working with exceptional people, solving meaningful problems, and eventually building businesses of my own.
